# Heart Emoji Colours Explained: What Does Each Heart Mean?

Someone ends a message with 💙 instead of ❤️, and suddenly you’re wondering whether the colour means something. Friendship? Romance? A polite way of keeping you at a distance?

**Heart emoji colours do not form a universal secret code.** They can express affection, support, a shared identity or simply a colour preference. Some associations are familiar within particular communities, but a heart’s colour alone cannot tell you what the sender feels.

That’s the useful starting point: notice the colour, then read the message.

## A quick guide to heart emoji colours

The names identify the colours, not a compulsory emotional meaning. Unicode’s [emoji FAQ](https://www.unicode.org/faq/emoji_dingbats.html) explains that colour words in names such as “blue heart” specify the recommended colour appearance.

Here are some ways you *might* use the different hearts. The examples are suggestions—not definitions everyone follows.

| Heart | Name | An example of how you could use it |
|---|---|---|
| ❤️ | Red heart | “Love you—see you soon ❤️” |
| 🩷 | Pink heart | “That was such a sweet surprise 🩷” |
| 🧡 | Orange heart | Add warmth or match an orange-themed caption. |
| 💛 | Yellow heart | “Thanks for brightening my day 💛” |
| 💚 | Green heart | Pair it with a message about a garden or a green team colour. |
| 💙 | Blue heart | Show affection or support for a team that wears blue. |
| 🩵 | Light blue heart | Match a sky-blue photo or send an affectionate message. |
| 💜 | Purple heart | Express affection or use a colour shared by your community. |
| 🤎 | Brown heart | Match a coffee-themed caption or express a personal connection. |
| 🖤 | Black heart | Add affection to a dark-coloured outfit or music caption. |
| 🤍 | White heart | “Thinking of you and your family 🤍” |
| 🩶 | Grey heart | Choose a muted colour for an affectionate or supportive message. |

**None of these examples makes a colour exclusive to that use.** You can send a red heart to a friend, a yellow heart to a partner or a blue heart simply because you like blue.

## Does a red heart always mean romantic love?

No. The **red heart emoji** can express romance, but also affection, gratitude, appreciation or support.

Compare:

- “I love you ❤️”
- “Thanks for checking on Mum ❤️”
- “Your dog is adorable ❤️”

The same heart fits three different relationships and situations. Emojipedia’s [analysis of heart emoji usage](https://blog.emojipedia.org/what-every-heart-emoji-really-means/) found red hearts alongside expressions of love, thanks and appreciation. That analysis used 2020 tweets; it is useful evidence of overlapping uses, not a current rulebook for private messages.

## Does a blue heart mean friendship or being “friend-zoned”?

A **blue heart** does not reliably announce someone’s romantic intentions—or the absence of them.

It might match a team colour, a brand, a photograph or the sender’s usual style. Trust and friendship can be meanings people attach to it, but you cannot decode “I only see you as a friend” from 💙 alone.

The same caution applies to **yellow and orange hearts**. Choosing 💛 or 🧡 does not guarantee that a message is platonic. If you want to make a relationship clear, words will do that more reliably than switching colours.

## Purple and green hearts can have community meanings

A colour can become a shared symbol within a fandom, team or group. Emojipedia’s historical analysis documented a strong association between **💜 and BTS fans**, and between **💚 and NCT fans**.

Those are examples of community context—not proof that everyone sending a purple or green heart belongs to those groups. The surrounding conversation may make the connection clear; the isolated heart may not.

If your friends already share a meaning for a colour, that shared understanding can matter more than a general emoji guide.

## What about black and white hearts?

It’s tempting to treat **🖤 black heart** as negative and **🤍 white heart** as positive. Real messages aren’t that tidy.

A black heart can be an affectionate choice that fits someone’s aesthetic. A white heart can accompany love, support or remembrance. Emojipedia’s analysis found remembrance-related language among white-heart uses, while also finding the broader affectionate language shared by other hearts.

Neither colour proves that a person is grieving, being sarcastic or expressing “pure love.” When the subject is sensitive, include the actual message:

> “I’m thinking of you. No need to reply 🤍”

The reassurance comes from the sentence—not from expecting the recipient to know a colour code.

## Heart shapes can add another layer

Colour isn’t the only difference. Some hearts include a visual effect or a changed shape:

| Emoji | A possible use |
|---|---|
| 💕 Two hearts | Affection or a warm connection |
| 💖 Sparkling heart | Affection with a decorative sparkle |
| 💔 Broken heart | Heartbreak, sadness or exaggerated disappointment |
| ❤️‍🩹 Mending heart | Recovery, comfort or healing |

These are still context-dependent. “The bakery sold out 💔” is not the same kind of heartbreak as a message about a breakup.

For more examples, explore [two hearts](https://emojispot.world/emoji/two-hearts/) and [sparkling heart](https://emojispot.world/emoji/sparkling-heart/).

## Which heart should you send?

Start with what you want to say, not a chart of supposedly safe colours.

1. **Write the message first.** “Thanks for being there” makes your gratitude clear.
2. **Consider your usual style together.** A heart you both use comfortably needs less explanation.
3. **Choose a colour you like or already share.** You don’t need a special reason.
4. **Be clearer when the stakes are higher.** An emoji is not a reliable substitute for discussing feelings or offering meaningful support.

App-generated badges are a separate case. If a heart appears automatically beside someone’s name, check that app’s explanation; it may have a defined product-specific meaning rather than the meaning of a heart someone typed.

## The takeaway

**Heart colours can add personality, but they cannot read someone’s mind for you.** Look at the words, the relationship and the setting before deciding what a heart means.

If you’re choosing one yourself, pick a heart that fits your message. You don’t have to solve a hidden code first.
