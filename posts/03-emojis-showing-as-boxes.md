# Why Are My Emojis Showing as Boxes or Question Marks?

A friend sends you a message, but one of the emojis appears as a blank square, a question mark or a strange group of symbols. Before you change settings, look closely at what is missing.

**An empty box often points to missing display support. A question mark can have several causes, and the replacement character � can indicate a text-decoding problem.** They are not all the same error, so there is no single “fix my emojis” switch.

Here’s how to narrow it down without resetting your phone or deleting your messages.

## First, identify what you’re seeing

| What appears? | A possible explanation | What to try first |
|---|---|---|
| An empty box or box with a symbol inside | The app or available font may not support the character. | Compare the same message in another app. |
| Several symbols instead of one emoji | The combined emoji sequence may be unsupported or incomplete. | Copy the complete emoji again. |
| A black-and-white symbol | The app may be using text presentation rather than colourful emoji presentation. | Check whether it remains recognisable and works elsewhere. |
| The symbol � | Text may have been replaced during decoding or conversion. | Ask the sender to resend the original text. |
| A plain question mark | It could be a substitute inserted by software—or simply a question mark. | Compare it with the sender’s original message. |

Unicode explains missing and unsupported emoji sequences in its [emoji specification](https://www.unicode.org/reports/tr51/). Its [encoding FAQ](https://www.unicode.org/faq/utf_bom.html) separately describes U+FFFD, the replacement character, as a possible response to invalid encoded text. You cannot reliably identify the original emoji from a generic replacement symbol alone.

## 1. Check whether the problem is limited to one app

Copy the message into another trusted app, such as your device’s notes app. If possible, ask the sender what they see too.

- **It works elsewhere on your device:** investigate the first app’s updates or rendering support.
- **It fails in several apps:** your device’s available emoji support may be involved, or the text itself may already be damaged.
- **Only one recipient has trouble:** their app or device may not support the character or sequence.

These are clues, not a diagnosis. Different apps can use different rendering systems, so one successful display does not prove that every other app is faulty.

## 2. Check for official app and system updates

New emoji support arrives through software updates—not merely because an emoji has been approved by Unicode. The [Unicode emoji FAQ](https://www.unicode.org/faq/emoji_dingbats.html) explains that vendors decide which characters to support and when to ship that support.

Check your device’s official software-update settings and the app’s official store listing. If an update is available, follow the provider’s instructions and any backup recommendations.

**Updating a keyboard alone may not fix what you see.** Choosing an emoji and displaying it are different jobs. A keyboard may let you insert a character that another app or recipient cannot render.

You do not need to start with a factory reset, an unofficial font installer or an unknown “emoji repair” app. Those are not sensible first steps for a missing symbol.

## 3. Copy the whole emoji, not just one piece

Some emoji are made from several characters. These can include skin-tone modifiers, presentation selectors or an invisible **zero-width joiner**, which helps form certain combined emoji. Unicode describes how these sequences work in its [emoji specification](https://www.unicode.org/reports/tr51/).

For example, 👩‍💻 may appear as a woman and a laptop separately if its combined form is unsupported or the sequence is altered.

Try selecting the emoji again from the keyboard or using a button that copies the complete sequence. Avoid manually deleting invisible characters to “clean up” the text: some of those characters are there for a reason.

Copying again can fix an incomplete selection. It will not add missing rendering support to an older app.

## 4. Treat � differently from a missing-glyph box

If text was replaced with **�**, installing a new font may not recover it. The original information may already have been lost during decoding or conversion.

Ask the sender to resend the message from the original source. If you manage the website or application involved, investigate its text encoding and storage rather than assuming this is only an emoji-font problem. Unicode’s [encoding FAQ](https://www.unicode.org/faq/utf_bom.html) explains the distinction.

For an ordinary conversation, you do not need to solve the technical cause yourself. “That emoji didn’t come through—what was it?” is often the quickest fix.

## 5. Use a clear alternative while you troubleshoot

If someone cannot see an emoji, write its name or meaning alongside it:

> “Congratulations! 🎉 — that’s a party popper.”

You can also choose another emoji you have confirmed they can see. If you share an image to show the intended design, add words describing it rather than relying on the picture alone.

That keeps the conversation understandable without promising that a particular emoji works on every device.

## Missing emoji keyboard? That’s a separate issue

If you can see emoji in messages but cannot find the keyboard, the problem is input rather than display support.

On iPhone or iPad, Apple’s instructions are **Settings → General → Keyboard → Keyboards → Add New Keyboard → Emoji**. See [Apple’s emoji keyboard guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102507). This adds the keyboard; it does not guarantee that every app or recipient can display every emoji.

## A useful rule of thumb

**Compare first, update through official channels, then recopy the complete emoji.** If the character was replaced or damaged, ask for the original again.

Most importantly, do not delete your messages or reset your device just because a single emoji appears as a box.
